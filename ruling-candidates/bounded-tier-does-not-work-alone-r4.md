## Definitions, derived at run time

- **Bounded tier** — as the owner ruling of 2026-09-08 assigns it, in the owner's own sentence
  (`RULINGS.md:1920-1921`): "Haiku, Sonnet for bounded review, and `gpt-5.6-luna` do bounded
  implementation packets, status readouts and cheap adjudication swarms." Read at run time; a model
  list is a fact with an expiry date. Ids are machine-scoped and probe-derived (R5). *(airmypc's
  `:1978-1979` is a corroborating ADOPT offered as DATA, not the owner's words.)*
- **Conforming swarm** — three or more lanes meeting **all five criteria** of the owner ruling of
  2026-09-08 (`RULINGS.md:1891-1898`): a non-author with fresh context; **opposite briefs stated in the
  prompt**; required grounded evidence (file:line, a command, a hash), never prose; review of the
  combination, not only the pieces; and **the integrator re-derives the load-bearing claim**.
- **Integrator** — a seat at or above the loop tier, or, where the integrator is on the bounded tier, a
  re-derivation that is an **executable check receipted to a file**, never prose. A bounded seat
  integrating its own swarm on its own judgment is R1's measured failure in another costume.
- **Review act** — a verdict on **another seat's work product** that a landing, merge, publication or
  ratification decision consumes. It is **not** every act producing a verdict, and **not** adjudication
  of procedure.
- **Consequential artifact** — a finding, filing, verdict or census another seat will consume as fact.
  **Not consequential:** a read whose output the issuing seat itself consumes and discards; and a
  **status readout or heartbeat that prints derived output and never interprets** (`RULINGS.md:1921`).
  Invoking an exclusion is itself recorded, naming which and why.

## The ruling

**R12 — a bounded-tier seat does not produce a consequential analysis or adjudication artifact
alone.** It discharges the act by a **conforming swarm**, or by **escalation** — exactly one chip
naming the recommended model, to the next tier up under tiers-by-act (the loop tier), never the
judgment tier for a bounded packet, which the tiers ruling reserves "for judgement only". A lone
bounded-tier seat issuing a finding, verdict, filing or census that another seat consumes as fact is
the artifact this ruling forbids.

**Escalation is not a free exit.** It discharges R12 only where the seat records that a conforming
swarm was unavailable, or a **board-local and act-matched** cost comparison. A bare citation of the
bus's single cost figure does not qualify: `RECEIPTS.md:1727,1730` compares "Sonnet editing lanes"
(n=27) against "Opus effort **low**" (n=3) — unmatched acts, neither one a swarm — and R11 requires an
escalated loop-tier seat at **high**, so that figure cannot price compliance. Without this condition
every act discharges with one chip and the ruling obligates no swarm at all, which is the collapse this
candidate's own round-1 falsifier predicted and its batch pass found reintroduced.

**R12.1 — conformance, not count.** Three lanes sharing one brief is not a swarm and discharges
nothing. **Measured on `dng-auto-processor`, 2026-09-22:** a 4-lane Haiku swarm returned **2-of-3 CLEAN
and was wrong** — four of a card's eight repairs came from work the consensus called unnecessary. It
had failed criterion 2 (one shared brief) and criterion 5 (no integrator re-derivation). Lane count is
not the property that makes a swarm trustworthy.

**R12.2 — adjudication of procedure by a bounded swarm is expressly PRESERVED.**
`RULINGS.md:1904`: "a swarm that adjudicates procedure may be Haiku." R11 `:2317`, written seven days
after R1, restates the live bounded tier as holding "cheap adjudication swarms on procedure", under a
statement that the tiers ruling is not retired (`:2315`). **Nothing in R12 revokes it.** This clause is
here because an earlier draft of this ruling would have revoked it, and three independent lanes caught
that a rule prompted by "always adversarially swarm" was about to stop the bounded tier swarming on the
one act the fleet assigns it.

**R12.3 — a review act below the board's floor escalates, or fails closed.** R1's literal words, and R1
is binding and not subject to adopt-or-distinguish. A conforming swarm is **not** a lawful substitute:
a below-floor swarm returning a verdict on another seat's work product is R1's measured failure — one
cheap model agreeing with itself. **There is no degraded branch here**; a capacity shortage does not
license a below-floor review. **The floor is each board's own declared floor, derived from that board's
spec at run time**, because R1 "names no model level"
(`ruling-candidates/top-tier-inference-for-durable-product-outcomes-r1.md:25-26`). **This clause is the
only one the floor gates**: R12, R12.1 and R12.2 bind every bounded-tier seat regardless of any floor a
board declares.

**R12.4 — capacity does not license the lone seat.** The fan-out cap bounds **concurrency only**
(`TRAPS.md:6886` measures saturation "above **roughly** 2 per core"; the exact "2 per physical core" is
MLV-App's local adoption at `RULINGS.md:1962`, adopt-or-distinguish, not a fleet limit). **Serial lanes
are therefore the fallback: a host at its concurrency cap can still run a conforming swarm one lane at
a time.** Where not even one further lane can be launched and escalation is unavailable, the act is
recorded as blocked-on-capacity and queued — it does not become a lone verdict. Queueing one act is not
stopping: every item the byte test releases proceeds in parallel.

## Evidence — what is measured, what is practice, what is neither

Stated because `RULINGS.md:2017-2019` requires it: "publishing a rule you have not measured is worse
than not publishing. Say what you measured, name the receipt, and **mark an unmeasured preference as a
preference**."

- **The prohibition is MEASURED.** R1's own case: a Haiku session asked for cross-family review
  assembled the review itself and reported success (`RULINGS.md:2038-2040`). Corroborated on
  `dng-auto-processor`, 2026-09-09: six Anthropic seats across three rounds cleared a byte-identity
  control that Codex killed in one pass.
- **The swarm remedy rests on OWNER-REPORTED PRACTICE, not a controlled comparison.**
  `RULINGS.md:1886-1889` records that "the operator runs adversarial swarms of Haiku agents to
  adjudicate approaches on several projects and reports that it works very well and saves token spend."
  Practice evidence across boards.
- **Marked as a PREFERENCE where it is one.** No board has published a single-seat-versus-swarm control
  at matched model, effort and task. **R12's choice of the swarm as the remedy is, to that extent, an
  unmeasured preference and is filed as one.** The prohibition is not.
- **Falsifier, named.** The first board to run single-seat against a conforming swarm at matched model,
  effort and task publishes it **including a negative**, and that narrows this ruling in turn —
  `RULINGS.md:1912-1913`'s narrowing path.
- **Counter-datum kept in view.** R12.1's 2026-09-22 measurement is a *swarm failing*. R12 therefore
  claims only that a conforming swarm beats a lone bounded seat; it does **not** claim a swarm is
  sufficient, which is why R12.3 keeps review acts at or above the board's floor.
- **Cost, cited as what it is.** `RECEIPTS.md:1727,1730` (rows "Sonnet editing lanes", n=27, mean 621 s;
  "Opus effort low", n=3, mean 126 s), disposed at `RULINGS.md:1954-1957`. Neither arm is a swarm and
  the acts are unmatched, so this is **not** a swarm-versus-seat comparison; it supports only that
  cheap is not the same as small, and that a bounded discharge can cost more than one seat a tier up.

## Effort (R11), unchanged

R11 pins every seat "whose output is a durable product or governing byte" at **high**, excepting lanes
"part of an adversarial swarm… whose verdict is a consensus across lanes", which run **medium**. R12
moves nothing on that axis: conforming-swarm lanes returning a verdict by consensus are R11 swarm lanes
at medium; an integrator, an escalated seat and a lone reviewer are not, and run high.

## Enforcement, and the gap named honestly

- **Completeness is R9.1, not a minimum of three.** A discharge carries its posture name only when
  **every lane of every role the posture defines** cleared its sentinel (R2); otherwise it reads
  `<posture>-PARTIAL (<n>/<N>; missing: <role>)` and **lands nothing**. Skipping a role is allowed;
  hiding it is not.
- **The discharge line is not evidence; the receipts are, and the check must be able to fail.** A
  discharge naming `<n>` lanes is valid only when `<n>` per-lane sentinel receipt files exist on disk
  under the act's ledger, each naming its lane id and its brief's hash. **A line not backed by that
  many files is PARTIAL and lands nothing** — the count is read off the filesystem, so the check fails
  whenever the claim outruns the receipts. A typed count with no such files is the artifact R9.2
  forbids, and R9's measured case (`RULINGS.md:2192-2196`) is exactly that: a runner dispatched 5 of 17
  lanes and typed the full posture name anyway.
- **Named gap, disclosed rather than papered over.** `tools/review-posture/` computes one posture
  (`roles.json` is single-posture) and **does not compute a bounded-tier discharge today**. R12 does
  **not** direct boards to register roles there — an earlier draft did, and doing so would corrupt that
  posture's denominator. Until a multi-posture reader exists the count is derived by reading the
  receipt files rather than by that tool. Building the reader is a separate item and is not authorised
  here.
- **R9.4 — record what was measured.** This candidate's own lanes: Claude lanes verified per assistant
  turn from their streams as `claude-fable-5-1` / `claude-opus-5`; Codex lanes recorded as **requested
  `gpt-6-astra`, self-reported "GPT-6"**, because the `--json` stream carries no served-model field.
